# Course Project | Investigating Mental Health Crisis with Survey Data
### Amelia Ubben | CS150

## Thesis
Are we actually facing a "mental health crisis?" While this dashboard does not claim to show definitive proof, it 
surely seems like we may be doing better than we think!

## Data Source
The data used here was sourced from [These Census Bureau Pulse Surveys](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/household-pulse-survey/data/datasets.2025.html#list-tab-1264157801).
</br> 
Data was downloaded from periods with distances of about 6 months when possible from 2020-2024.
I merged these survey results to include the columns I wanted, the corresponding date periods, and used the specified 
weight of each response before displaying the data.
</br> Please note that a great portion of this project was data searching and processing. Health data is very privatized so 
public use data is hard to come by, and survey data is difficult to synthesize and display.

## Support Article
[Here is a source article that supports my thesis](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/parenting-is-not-a-fad/202408/are-we-or-are-we-not-in-a-youth-mental-health-crisis)
</br>
I used claims in this article to support the fact that we may not actually be experiencing a mental health crisis in the
way media is pushing, which is outlined in my slidedeck

## Results 
This dashboard shows that general self-reported mental health is not actually declining, but has been 
generally improving since the pandemic in 2020. Check it out yourself to see how responses have changed 
over time!

### Further Context
What are the cultural factors that are inflating the mental health crisis? </br>
Over-interpretation of emotions as disorders, risky messaging of wellness industry, and misleading statistics

What is the "prevalence inflation hypothesis"? </br>
This states that prevalence of mental health is due to more reports
and not higher occurrences due to increased awareness of symptoms.
</br>
It is important to be aware of this kind of messaging because the wellness industry can make a lot more money
if people thing they need to buy some form of cure.
</br>
Disclaimer: This project has no intention of completely discrediting the reality of a mental health crisis, 
nor does it in any way discredit the very real issues of mental illness that people may experience.
